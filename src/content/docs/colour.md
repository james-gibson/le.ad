# The colour, and why it is measured

The brief was four things that fight each other: a **muted steelblue** wall, a **subtle gold
noise** over it, **white smoke** for ink, and a **sharp hot pink** accent — with clear contrast
ratios and the bright contrast diffused. A pink that sharp on a wall that muted is exactly
where an unmeasured palette goes wrong, so the palette is a file and the build reads it.

## The wall

```text
ink        #2E4A5F   the wall
ink-deep   #263F52   a recessed band
panel      #223949   panels, darker than the wall
panel-2    #1B2F3E   wells, darkest
```

Everything is *darker* than the wall. That is the diffusion: there is no raised card, no white
surface, no second brightest thing competing with the smoke. A panel is a recess, which is what
a desk actually is.

## The smoke

```text
smoke      #EDF2F4   8.23:1 on the wall, 10.64:1 on a panel
smoke-dim  #C3D1DA   5.95:1 on the wall, 7.69:1 on a panel
```

Not `#FFFFFF`. A cool off-white reads as smoke rather than light, and the body floor is 7:1
rather than the 4.5:1 WCAG asks for, because the wall is textured and a ratio computed against
a flat colour should leave headroom for the grain.

The literal smoke is the drifting element: three blurred radial gradients at 5–16% opacity,
under the ink, animated over 64–96 seconds. It is the only unasked-for movement on the page,
and `prefers-reduced-motion` stops it dead.

## The gold

```text
gold       #C9A45C
gold-soft  #E0C48C
```

Gold is declared **decorative** and is never allowed to carry text. It appears twice: as a wash
in the far corner of the wall, and as an SVG turbulence field tinted through an `feColorMatrix`
and laid on with `mix-blend-mode: soft-light`. There is no gold image to download; the grain is
generated by the browser from two numbers and a matrix.

## The pink

```text
pink       #FF2E88   the fill — a solid, never a small glyph
pink-ink   #FF94C1   4.54:1 on the wall; links, marks, focus rings
pink-deep  #C41A63   pressed
night      #12202B   4.73:1 on the pink fill
```

This is the interesting part. `#FF2E88` on `#2E4A5F` is **2.65:1** — sharp, and unreadable. So
the sharp pink is only ever a *fill*: a button's background, a badge's background, a lease bar,
a two-pixel border. The text that sits on it is near-black `#12202B` at 4.73:1. The pink that
carries meaning as *ink* — a link, a focus ring, the lamp — is the lighter `#FF94C1`, which is
still unmistakably hot pink and clears 4.5:1 on every ground.

That is the whole trick, and it is why the palette is measured: the accent did not get weaker,
it got **assigned**. The sharp colour is in the places where area does the work.

## What the gate does

`scripts/contrast.mjs` reads `src/lib/palette.json` and:

1. checks every declared pair against its floor;
2. audits the built HTML for an ink at partial opacity — `text-x/70` counted as `x` is how a
   palette passes review and fails a reader;
3. reports the tightest pair it found, so a passing palette still says how much room it has.

`scripts/tokens.mjs` closes the other door: every `--le-*` custom property in
`src/ui/theme.css` must equal the token of the same name in `palette.json`, so the stylesheet
and the measurement cannot drift. A colour that is not in the file is not a colour this site
may paint.

The `/palette` route prints the same table, with the same numbers, from the same JSON.
